Overview

Let the policy's own rollouts decide which action to execute.

A VLA's sampled action chunks vary in quality, and large VLM verifiers are too slow to check them at every query. HDH trains a compact verifier on the terminal outcomes of the frozen policy's past rollouts. At each query it scores two sampled candidates and the robot executes the more promising one.

Read the abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have made significant progress in general-purpose robotic manipulation. However, sampled action sequences can be ineffective, while evaluating alternatives with computationally expensive verifiers adds latency at each policy query. In this work, we propose HDH, a lightweight framework that uses hindsight from a frozen VLA's own deployments to improve action verification. The framework has three stages. First, we deploy the policy and collect its autonomous rollouts. We then train a compact action verifier to assign each action chunk a predicted task-success score. Finally, the frozen VLA generates action candidates, and the verifier selects the candidate with the highest score for execution. Across four LIBERO-10 tasks, HDH achieves an average success rate of 82.3% and on three real-world manipulation tasks, HDH improves average success over the baseline policy by 22.6%.

We collect autonomous rollouts of the frozen policy and use their terminal success and failure to train a compact verifier (left). At each query, the verifier scores sampled action chunks from the current observation and execution history (center), and the robot executes the highest-scoring candidate (right).

Method

Learn from outcomes, then choose among samples.

The VLA is never updated. HDH changes which sampled action is executed, not how candidates are generated.

Stage 01

Collect

Deploy the frozen policy with one sampled chunk per query and record its autonomous rollouts with their terminal success or failure.

Stage 02

Train

Label every query of an episode with that episode's outcome and train a compact verifier to predict eventual task success of the executed prefix. Splits are made at the episode level.

Stage 03

Select

At each query, draw K = 2 candidates with independently seeded flow-matching noise, score both, and execute the first H = 8 actions of the highest-scoring one.

(a) Given the instruction, images and proprioception, (b) we sample candidate action chunks from a frozen VLA. (c) A compact verifier trained on terminal rollout outcomes assigns each candidate a predicted task-success score. (d) The robot executes the first H actions of the best candidate.

What the verifier sees

  1. SigLIP prefix tokens of both cameras, taken from the frozen VLA and pooled to 8 × 8 per view (128 tokens)
  2. An 8-D proprioceptive token and a constant per-task token
  3. The candidate's executed prefix (H × 7 actions) as action tokens that self-attend, then cross-attend to the context
  4. HDH only: a GRU history token over up to three previous queries' observations, states and executed actions
